DC Potentials and Ultra-Slow Electrical Activity

An overview of DC potentials, how they differ from more familiar electrophysiological signals and how Neuro-Amea records them.

01

What DC Potentials Are

Direct-current (DC) potentials are slow shifts in electrical potential recorded from the scalp. They differ from the faster oscillatory activity usually examined with conventional EEG.

02

Electrical Activity at a Different Time Scale

DC potentials vary on a much slower time scale than conventional EEG rhythms — from seconds to minutes or longer. The physiological processes behind these shifts are complex and remain an active area of research.

03

DC Potentials and EEG

DC-potential recording and conventional EEG focus on different time scales and require different recording approaches. The two measurements are not interchangeable.

04

What Generates DC Potentials?

06

How Neuro-Amea Records Them

Neuro-Amea records DC potentials non-invasively from defined scalp locations. The software processes the measurements and presents them as quantitative values and visual outputs.

07

Brain State Is Dynamic

Recorded values can vary over time and under different recording conditions. Repeated measurements make it possible to compare recordings collected at defined time points.

08

Turning Measurements Into Patterns

Channel readings can be presented as spatial and interhemispheric patterns, so a specialist can review them together rather than as isolated values.

09

What the Measurement Does — and Does Not — Tell Us

A recorded DC potential value is a physiological measurement. On its own, it does not establish a diagnosis or the cause of an observed pattern.
